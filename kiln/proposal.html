The proposal

The Drying Cycle

Keep the kiln's organisation; change how it decides. The nuts stay where they are loaded and the heated air's route moves, but the system moves on when the nuts are ready, not when a clock says so.

In the story, the kiln is rebuilt into this dryer one change at a time. Here: how it would work, why, and what would prove it wrong. In the research it is drafted as A Dryer on Schedule (draft 5).

What this is

A drying system for arecanut in wet spells, informed by the operating logic of the tile kiln at Kundapur, for a group of neighbouring growers to share.

What it is not

A Hoffmann kiln for farms. No ring of masonry, no fire in contact with the nuts, no fixed two-hour clock. It does not claim the dryer works.

    01 · The problem

    Two problems, not one

    When rain arrives while arecanut is drying, two different things go wrong. Growers already solve the first. The second is what this proposal is for.

          03 · How it would work

          The nuts stay put; the air's route moves

          Four bays in a row, one fixed heater and fan, twelve dampers. Four dampers are open at a time. To move on, three close and three open, and the route shifts one bay along. Step through it.

          The full damper table

          Make before break: on every step the new dampers open before the old ones shut, so the fan never pushes against a closed system. The row is straight; the return duct R41 closes it into a loop, so any bay can come first. A U-shaped row would keep that duct short.

          04 · Is the air fit for the next bay?

          Passing air on is only useful until it isn't

          As air dries nuts it cools and fills with water. Passed to fresh, cold nuts too late, it would condense on them. So before each bay the system checks two things: can the air still take up water, and is its dew point below the nuts' temperature?

          Air going in

          Why relative humidity alone misleads

          Relative humidity says how close air is to saturation at its present temperature. Two streams at the same relative humidity can differ a great deal: at 60%, air at 30 °C holds about 16 g of water per kg and has a dew point near 21 °C; at 45 °C it holds about 37 g/kg and its dew point is near 35 °C. What matters is the humidity ratio, the dew point, and how much more the air could take up as it cools by evaporation.

          05 · Two clocks

          Same organisation, different decisions

          The kiln is time-triggered: every two hours the fire moves on, without fail. The dryer would be condition-triggered: it reads the air, decides where the air may go, and moves on when the nuts are ready. Copying the kiln's clock would be copying a rule without its reason.

            A person stays in the loop at step 5: the sensors suggest, the operator's samples confirm, using the test growers already use. The kiln has no judgement in its timing; the dryer places judgement in its timing on purpose. Every threshold is open. U
