01 · Lineage

An idea that travelled, and changed shape on the way

Hoffmann's kiln was copied around the world within forty years. It reached the Mangalore coast as part of a new tile industry, and it is one of the last of its kind still firing there.

      03 · The air's route

      One way in, one way out

      The saving does not come from a better fire. It comes from the order in which the air meets the tiles. Here the loop is unrolled into a line, so the whole route can be followed.

        What an energy audit of this tile cluster proposed

        Between 2008 and 2012, India's Bureau of Energy Efficiency audited tile kilns in the Mangalore–Kundapura cluster. Its reports found exhaust leaving the kilns at about 350–400 °C, and no temperature measurement anywhere in the cluster: firing was held by the stokers' skill. Its first proposal was a movable fan that pulls the exhaust through more chambers of unfired tiles before it leaves.

        In this page's terms: let the air cross more tiles ahead before the open damper. The savings in the reports are projections. No evidence was found that any of them were carried out.

        L BEE (2012), detailed project reports for the Mangalore tile cluster: SAMEEEKSHA directory.

        04 · Two readings

        One chamber's life, or one moment of the whole kiln

        Every chamber goes through the same firing, two hours behind the one before it. Read the table below down a column and you get one chamber's life; read it across a row and you get the whole kiln at one moment, every chamber at a different stage.

        Columns are chambers 1–8; rows are hours 0–16, one full loop. Colour is the model's heat. Use the arrow keys to move.

        S Simulated: one loop of the model, on the two-hour interval recorded at the factory. Heat is on the model's own 0–1 scale, not a temperature. The only temperature from the factory is the 850–900 °C peak.
